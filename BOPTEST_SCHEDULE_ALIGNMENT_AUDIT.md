# BESTEST Air schedule alignment audit

**G218 remains ENERGY PASS / COMFORT FAIL.** In pinned BOPTEST 0.9.0, the thermostat is scheduled to relax at 18:00, while the packaged comfort resource keeps occupied bounds until 18:10. The original scoring code reproduced the archived result. This audit found no G218 scoring or timestamp defect to correct.

**Why the clocks differ.** The source generator derives the companion occupancy from positive simulated latent gains and exports the baseline thermostat outputs as bounds. At duplicate timestamps it normally drops the earlier row. At exactly 18:00 it drops the later row, retaining the pre-transition values. The shipped CSV matches the source mirror numerically and shows the resulting pattern on all **365 days**. It begins the occupied schedule at 08:00 and keeps **two people, 21/24°C and 90 W latent gain** at 18:00. The next row on G218 day 1 is **18:10**, with **zero people, 15/30°C and zero latent gain**. The intermediate physical load changes follow the Modelica schedules. The original annual generator input and the rationale for the special case are unavailable in the pinned tree. The delivered resource establishes what the benchmark scores. Times are elapsed simulation seconds from the start of the benchmark year. The model uses a fixed 86,400-second daily clock and the loader uses a 365-day year without time-zone or daylight-saving conversion. The loader uses each resource's first sample spacing to select the common grid, which is **600 s** here. It takes the last resource row at or before each grid point and repeats that lookup at requested timestamps. Exact timestamps use the row at that time. The pinned loader reproduced the occupancy, bound, and gain forecasts at all **864** issued origins in each arm. G218 results use **30 s** native samples and **300 s** control advances. On day 1 the last occupied thermostat readback was at **18:00:00 (18,900,000 s)** and the first setback readback at **18:00:30 (18,900,030 s)** with overrides off. The scored bounds changed at **18:10:00 (18,900,600 s)**. ## Score verification and interpretation

| Discomfort over 72 hours, K·h | Native | Autonomous |

| Official, recomputed with the pinned KPI code | 1.104554737586 | 2.240787055477 |
| Project, independently recomputed with the frozen bounds | 0.052413884998 | 0.053099987617 |

Both official totals matched the archived API values. The project totals matched the sealed pair within numerical precision. Both arms exceeded 26°C at **18:05:00 through 18:09:30 on day 1** (18,900,300–18,900,570 s inclusive, every 30 s). The violation counter uses the opening sample, so **ten** intervals count toward its **five minutes**. The KPI uses the mean of the two endpoint excesses. That adds the leading interval **18:04:30–18:05:00** and includes the trailing interval **18:09:30–18:10:00**, whose closing endpoint scores zero under the wider unoccupied band. Those **eleven** trapezoids account for the entire **+0.000686102619 K·h**. [Sealed comparison](SDP_BOPTEST_Pilot/autonomous_hvac_stage1/g218_runtime_state/batches/G218_STAGE1_NATIVE_AUTONOMOUS/PAIRED_VALIDATION.json)

The shared setback let both rooms warm during the last scored occupied minutes. On day 1 the autonomous temperature gap was **+0.502°C** at the 11:45 release, **−0.00012°C** at the end of the recovery window and **−0.000019°C** at 18:00. Before setback, autonomous cooling still drew about **2 W** more at nearly the same room temperature. After setback, the gap reached **+0.00936°C** by 18:09:30. Days 2 and 3 showed similar gaps at 18:09:30 (**+0.01049°C** and **+0.01117°C**), with both arms below 26°C. The pattern makes it plausible that the morning hold affected the state entering the evening transition. It cannot distinguish retained heat, equipment or controller state, or numerical divergence. The common schedule explains the timing of the excursion, while the measured temperature gap explains the extra scored discomfort.

## Smallest justified next step

**Retain Stage 1 now (C) and gather evidence before changing it (D).** The schedule question is resolved offline. Before changing the controller, use archived trajectories to check whether observations available before the 11:00 decision can predict late-day comfort risk. The archived 30-minute forecast ends well before the evening transition. If that check supports a risk screen, a single guard that shortens or skips the morning hold on high-risk days is a candidate. Verifying that it preserves both energy savings and comfort would require a fresh, separately authorized matched comparison under the original criteria.
